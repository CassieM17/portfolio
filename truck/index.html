To Truck or not to Truck?

Timeframe2025
ProjectMr. Bulgogi
RoleData Analyst
ToolsGoogle Sheets · NPV Modeling · Survey Research

Summary

Mr. Bulgogi is a Korean food truck that shows up on Carnegie Mellon's campus a few weeks a semester and reliably sells out. For a Decision Analysis course, I worked with a team of four to answer the question CMU Dining Services actually has to answer: should Mr. Bulgogi stay a seasonal pop-up, become a permanent restaurant, or stay a pop-up while a competing Korean restaurant opens permanently instead? I led the data side, designing the student survey, building the Even Swaps and NPV models, and pressure-testing the recommendation against the assumptions it depended on.

The Decision

The decision sits with CMU Dining Services, weighing three real objectives at once: student satisfaction, building a base of regular customers, and profit. Three alternatives were on the table:

Go permanent

Establish Mr. Bulgogi as a full-time, on-campus restaurant.

Stay the course

Keep Mr. Bulgogi exactly as it is: a seasonal food truck, nothing added.

Pop-up + competitor

Keep the truck seasonal, but let a new Korean restaurant open permanently alongside it.

Turning Opinions Into Dollars

We ran a 104-respondent student survey and went directly to the sources for operating data, emailing Mr. Bulgogi's owner for cost figures and CMU Dining Services for contract and space-availability context. Student satisfaction isn't naturally priced, so we used Even Swaps to convert it into dollar terms: we asked what students would be willing to pay for a bowl, for eating weekly, for the option to eat there again, and for a "significant," "moderate," or "slight" improvement in satisfaction, then applied those rates against each alternative's projected traffic. That let satisfaction sit inside the same financial model as rent, ingredients, and revenue, rather than being a bullet point next to it.

Each alternative's monetized cash flows were then projected over a 20-year horizon and discounted using CMU's own weighted average cost of capital (7.01%), calculated from the university's 2024 financial statements: the same discount rate a university treasurer would actually use, not a generic assumption.

What the Model Found

Going permanent wins by a wide margin. Its 20-year NPV comes in roughly ten times higher than either staying seasonal or ceding the permanent slot to a new competitor, driven less by the extra open days themselves than by unmet demand the survey surfaced directly: over half of respondents said they wanted Mr. Bulgogi but it simply wasn't open when they were looking, and a majority said a full-time location would meaningfully improve their satisfaction with campus dining.

Stress-Testing the Recommendation

A model is only as trustworthy as its shakiest assumption, so we built two-way sensitivity tables (discount rate against improvement rate, and improvement rate against carryover rate) spanning a wide range around our base-case estimates. Going permanent stayed the top choice in every single combination we tested. That mattered more than the headline number: it meant the recommendation didn't depend on us guessing the exact right inputs, just the right direction.

Recommendation

We recommended that CMU Dining Services open Mr. Bulgogi as a permanent, full-time restaurant on campus. It's projected to substantially outearn either alternative over a 20-year horizon, the result holds up under a wide range of assumptions, and it directly answers demand the survey data made hard to ignore: students who already wanted this and couldn't get it.

Assumptions & Key Uncertainties

What the model assumed: that a full-time location sells more bowls per day than the truck does (based on the share of survey respondents who said they'd eat more often if Mr. Bulgogi were open full-time, applied as an improvement rate over current weekly demand); that a new competing restaurant would land at roughly 90% of Mr. Bulgogi's satisfaction with students, with some of that demand carrying over rather than being lost outright; and that total revenue scales with bowls sold times customers, with some natural variability from add-ons, appetizers, and desserts.

What we couldn't fully resolve: whether students are actually as unsatisfied with current dining as the survey suggests, versus just enthusiastic about a truck they already like; whether a permanent location would genuinely raise satisfaction or just formalize an existing favorite; how daily order volume really shifts between a temporary and permanent format once the novelty wears off; the risk that a permanent Mr. Bulgogi cannibalizes traffic from other campus dining options rather than growing the pie; and how much of the revenue CMU actually keeps, since contract terms and margin splits with dining partners weren't fully available to us.

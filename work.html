Project 01 — Opus
Personal Product Design Project
Opus — Building a Trusted Marketplace for Live Musicians

How might we make it easier and more trustworthy for musicians to discover, secure, manage, and get paid for live performance opportunities?

RoleProduct Designer, UX Researcher, Product Strategist, Information Architect, Database Designer
TypePersonal Project
ToolsFigma · Claude · GitHub

The Problem

Finding live performance work is surprisingly difficult for freelance musicians. Unlike industries with centralized job boards, musicians rely on fragmented networks — Facebook groups, Instagram, word of mouth, professors, orchestra directors, email lists, venue websites — to discover opportunities. Because hiring is relationship-driven, talented musicians who are new to a city or don't know the right people struggle to find consistent work. Even after landing a gig, communication, contracts, scheduling, and payment are typically scattered across multiple apps.

Research Findings

I examined the hiring workflow from both sides of the marketplace.

Musicians struggle to

  • Find gigs matching their instrument and experience
  • Determine whether opportunities are legitimate
  • Build credibility with unfamiliar organizers
  • Track communication before performances
  • Receive timely payment

Organizers struggle to

  • Find qualified performers quickly
  • Evaluate applicants beyond a résumé
  • Determine whether someone is reliable
  • Fill openings before event dates
  • Manage communication with multiple musicians

This revealed that the challenge wasn't simply job discovery — it was building trust between both sides.

The Opportunity

Most gig platforms function as job boards, but music hiring is different — organizers often choose performers because they've worked together before, a colleague recommended them, or a professor vouched for them. Rather than replacing professional networking, I designed a platform that embraces it: Opus, a trusted marketplace combining networking, professional portfolios, and gig management in one place.

Key Features

Professional Profiles

Headshot, instruments, experience, genres, recordings, availability, rates, and reviews — helping organizers evaluate beyond a résumé.

Ensemble Profiles

String quartets, jazz trios, bands, and chamber groups can be booked as a complete unit instead of contacting each musician separately.

Smart Gig Board

Detailed postings — date, budget, instruments, dress code, repertoire — filterable by location, instrument, genre, and compensation.

Trust Through Networks

Organizers see mutual connections, shared ensembles, and past collaborations — rewarding real professional relationships.

Integrated Messaging

Built-in chat with hire confirmation, creating a reliable record without conversations scattered across apps.

Payment Tracking

Organizers mark musicians as paid post-performance, giving both sides visibility into outstanding compensation.

Information Architecture & Database Design

I modeled the relationships connecting users, profiles, ensembles, gigs, applications, recommendations, and professional networks, then translated that model into a relational database — Users, Profiles, Network Connections, Ensembles, Ensemble Members, Gigs, Applications, Media, and Recommendations — to validate that every feature could be supported by a scalable backend.

Design Principles

  • Build trust — recommendations, shared connections, and past collaborations help organizers hire with confidence.
  • Reduce friction — discovery, applications, messaging, scheduling, and payment tracking happen in one place.
  • Support real hiring behavior — Opus makes existing professional relationships visible and useful, rather than ignoring them.

Reflection

This project deepened my understanding of designing marketplace products that serve two distinct user groups with different goals, and gave me hands-on experience translating product requirements into information architecture and relational database models. Next steps I'd explore: built-in payment processing, verified references, calendar sync, contract generation, AI-powered recommendations, and reputation scoring.
